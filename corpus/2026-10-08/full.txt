THE READ
The Committee's September 16 move to 3.75–4.00% keeps the front end anchored, and the minutes released Wednesday did nothing to unwind it. Inflation is still above target — August headline CPI 3.4% and August core CPI 2.4%, with August headline PCE 3.4% and August core PCE 3.0% — while the labor side has cooled: September payrolls +29,000, a September unemployment rate of 4.2% and September average hourly earnings +3.0% over the year, on Q2 2026 GDP of +2.2% (third estimate). That split — firm prices, softening jobs, a Committee still tightening — is the tension on the screen this morning. At the October 7 close the front end eased a touch — the 2-year at 4.77%, two basis points lower than the prior day, and the 1-year at 4.42%, down four — while the long end firmed, the 30-year at 5.67% and the 20-year at 5.71%, each three basis points higher on the day. The curve steepened modestly: 2s10s at +51 bp, three wider on the day, and 5s30s at +64 bp, also three wider, with 3M10Y unchanged at +106 bp. The 20-year prints four basis points above the 30-year. As arithmetic, the 2-year sits 89 bp above the 3.88% effective funds rate. For the illustrative book below, higher long-end par yields lower the market value of fixed-rate AFS securities and deepen unrealized marks, while the roughly $125M of securities cash flow due over the next twelve months turns over at new-money levels well above the 2.95% book yield. Both are facts the next EVE and AOCI run will carry; the figures that follow size each one, and none of this is a view on what to do with the portfolio.
