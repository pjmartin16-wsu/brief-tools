A hiking Fed, a 2s10s slope of +44 bp and September CPI on Wednesday.

THE READ
The Federal Reserve raised the federal funds target range by a quarter point to 3.75–4.00% on Sep 16, a 12–0 vote, and the September projections put the median federal funds rate at 4.1% for the end of 2026 (central tendency 4.1–4.4%). August headline CPI ran 3.4% and core CPI 2.4%; August headline PCE ran 3.4% and core PCE 3.0%; September nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000 and the September unemployment rate was 4.2%. September CPI is due Wednesday, Oct 14, the week's main data point.

Treasury's Oct 9 close put the 2-year at 4.80%, the 10-year at 5.24% and the 30-year at 5.60%, leaving 2s10s at +44 bp, 3 bp flatter than the day before. On the week, the 10-year is down 4 bp and the 2-year down 3 bp, while the 3-month bill is up 6 bp at 4.25%. Overnight money sits inside the range: EFFR printed 3.88% on Oct 8, with IORB at 3.90%.

For a deposit-funded balance sheet the week's move is small but two-sided. Slightly lower intermediate yields lift AFS market values at the margin and trim new-money yields by the same few basis points; both show up in the next AOCI and EVE run. On the funding side, the top 12-month CD APY on Bankrate's list is 4.75%, above the effective funds rate of 3.88%, so renewal betas on maturing CDs are being tested against a retail bid above overnight wholesale money.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOUR BALANCE SHEET
- Deposits. Renewal betas are now being tested against a 4.75% top 12-month CD APY on Bankrate's list while SOFR sits at 3.87%. What a treasury team would measure: how much of the maturing CD book renews above the effective funds rate, and what that does to the marginal cost of deposits.
- Investments. On the week the 5-year is down 4 bp and the 10-year down 4 bp: new-money yields are marginally lower and AFS market values marginally higher. Both show up in the next AOCI and EVE run.
- IRR / ALCO. The Sep 16 hike and a +44 bp 2s10s slope set the base case for the next NII and EVE run. Measure repricing gaps against an effective funds rate of 3.88% and the 2-year at 4.80%, and test whether modeled deposit betas match realized betas since the hike; for credit unions the same move feeds the NEV and 5300 figures.
