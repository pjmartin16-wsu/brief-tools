Treasury Full Desk Read
A quarter-point hike into still-elevated inflation, and a curve that steepened on the week — what it does to a deposit-funded book.
Tuesday, October 6, 2026 · Market data as of Oct 5 close · Fed funds 3.75%–4.00% (raised Sep 16)
The Read

The FOMC raised the federal funds target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16, by a 12–0 vote, with the statement calling inflation “elevated” and framing the move as support for a “timelier return” to the 2 percent goal. Inflation has not cooperated: August headline CPI ran 3.4% and core CPI 2.4%, while August headline PCE ran 3.4% and core PCE 3.0%. The September Summary of Economic Projections carried a median year-end 2026 funds rate of 4.1% (central tendency 4.1–4.4%).

Against that backdrop the Treasury curve steepened on the week. Measured against the September 28 close five business days earlier, the front end fell — the 1-year off 12 bp to 4.47% and the 2-year off 8 bp to 4.84% — while the long end rose, the 10-year up 7 bp to 5.31% and the 30-year up 10 bp to 5.66%. That widened 2s10s from +32 bp to +47 bp. The front-end decline followed a soft September payrolls print (+29,000, released October 2); the long end reflects inflation still above target and the term structure of supply. The 2-year sits 96 bp above the 3.88% effective funds rate.

For a deposit-funded book the week did two things at once. Higher long-end yields lower the market value of fixed-rate available-for-sale securities, so an AFS portfolio carries a modestly deeper unrealized mark than a week ago; at the same time, cash flowing off the book rolls into higher new-money yields than it carried. Both are facts to measure in the next EVE and AOCI run, not a single direction. Deposit competition has not eased: the top advertised 12-month CD on Bankrate’s list is 4.50% and the top advertised savings rate 4.25%.

Macro prints: August headline CPI 3.4%, core CPI 2.4%; August headline PCE 3.4%, core PCE 3.0%; September payrolls +29,000, unemployment 4.2%, average hourly earnings +3.0% y/y; Q2 2026 real GDP +2.2% (third estimate).

Today’s Tie-In
Securities cash flow
About $125M rolls off the book over the next year. Each 25 bp between the 2.95% book yield and whatever that cash earns next is on the order of $313k a year, gross, full-year, full deployment ($125M × 0.25%).
CD maturities
The $155M Q3 tranche at ~4.35% reprices against the sample bank’s own 4.00–4.10% renewal defense band. Renewing 25–35 bp below the tranche rate is ~$0.4–0.5M a year on that tranche.
AOCI
The mark sits at −$41.4M, up $4.6M over three months (less negative). AOCI sensitivity is about $4.3M per 25 bp, taken at the whole securities portfolio’s 4.2-year effective duration (the book states no AFS-only duration); with the long end higher on the week the mark is modestly lower.
Interest-rate risk
EVE at +300 is −14.6%, beyond the internal −13.75% watch trigger but within the −25% policy limit; NII year-1 runs +2.1% at +100 and −2.4% at −100, inside the ±5% limit.

Treasury Desk Watch
1.Model the next EVE and AOCI run on the current curve: the long end is up 7 bp on the week and 10 bp at the 30-year, so the AFS mark and the +300 EVE both deserve a fresh read against the −13.75% watch trigger.
2.Measure the gap between the 2.95% securities book yield and current new-money levels on the ~$125M of securities cash flow due over the next year; size the full-year earnings swing per 25 bp ($313k) rather than assuming a pickup.
3.Scenario-test the $155M Q3 CD tranche at ~4.35% against the 4.00–4.10% renewal defense band and against the 4.50% top advertised CD APY on Bankrate’s list; quantify the annual cost of each renewal beta.
4.Compare the cost of deposits (1.92%, beta 34% vs 45% modeled) with the deposit-competition table; identify which core segments are most exposed if advertised yields hold at 4.25–4.50%.
5.Evaluate how the September 16 hike is working through funding: the discount window stands at 4.00% and the effective funds rate at 3.88%; gauge how much of the 25 bp has reached the deposit book versus the asset side.
6.Track this week’s calendar — the August trade balance (Oct 6) and next week’s September CPI (Oct 14) and PPI (Oct 15) — as inputs to the October 27–28 FOMC, and map each to the book’s rate-risk measurement, not to a position.
