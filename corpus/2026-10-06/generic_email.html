Treasury Morning Brief
A quarter-point hike into still-elevated inflation, and a curve that steepened on the week.
Tuesday, October 6, 2026 · Market data as of Oct 5 close · Fed funds 3.75%–4.00% (raised Sep 16)
The Read

The FOMC raised the federal funds target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16, by a 12–0 vote, with the statement calling inflation “elevated” and framing the move as support for a “timelier return” to the 2 percent goal. Inflation has not cooperated: August headline CPI ran 3.4% and core CPI 2.4%, while August headline PCE ran 3.4% and core PCE 3.0%. The September Summary of Economic Projections carried a median year-end 2026 funds rate of 4.1% (central tendency 4.1–4.4%).

Against that backdrop the Treasury curve steepened on the week. Measured against the September 28 close five business days earlier, the front end fell — the 1-year off 12 bp to 4.47% and the 2-year off 8 bp to 4.84% — while the long end rose, the 10-year up 7 bp to 5.31% and the 30-year up 10 bp to 5.66%. That widened 2s10s from +32 bp to +47 bp. The front-end decline followed a soft September payrolls print (+29,000, released October 2); the long end reflects inflation still above target and the term structure of supply. The 2-year sits 96 bp above the 3.88% effective funds rate.

For any deposit-funded balance sheet the move cuts two ways. Higher long-end yields lower the market value of fixed-rate AFS securities, so AFS marks and AOCI move the wrong way as the long end backs up; meanwhile new-money yields on maturing assets are higher than the rates they replace. Both show up in the next EVE and AOCI run. On the liability side, renewal betas are still being tested against a 4.50% top 12-month CD APY and a 4.25% top savings APY on Bankrate’s advertised lists — consumer yields, not a wholesale cost of funds.

Macro prints: August headline CPI 3.4%, core CPI 2.4%; August headline PCE 3.4%, core PCE 3.0%; September payrolls +29,000, unemployment 4.2%, average hourly earnings +3.0% y/y; Q2 2026 real GDP +2.2% (third estimate).

Commentary
Overnight & the Curve
The effective funds rate held at 3.88% and SOFR at 3.88% (Oct 2 prints), with the 30-day average SOFR at 3.78% (eff Oct 5), after the September 16 hike to 3.75%–4.00%. The curve steepened on the week as the front end fell and the long end rose; 2s10s widened to +47 bp, 3M10Y stood at +109 bp and 5s30s at +60 bp (Oct 5 close). The 2-year sits 96 bp above the effective funds rate.
Funding & Deposit Market
Advertised deposit competition held firm — a 4.50% top 12-month CD and a 4.25% top savings APY on Bankrate’s lists (consumer yields, not a cost of funds). The discount window primary credit rate is 4.00%. FHLB advance and brokered CD rates are not shown: neither has a published primary-source quote for Oct 5’s close. Mortgage rates rose, the 30-year fixed at 7.28% (Freddie Mac, Oct 1).
What It Means for Your Balance Sheet
Deposits
Renewal betas are now being tested against a 4.50% top 12-month CD APY and a 4.25% top savings APY on Bankrate’s advertised lists. Measure the share of core and CD balances repricing into year-end and the cost-of-funds path that implies.
Investments
New-money yields are higher and AFS market values are lower as the long end backs up: both show up in the next EVE and AOCI run. Measure the unrealized mark and the gap between what maturing assets yield and what they earned before, rather than treating either in isolation.
IRR & ALCO
With the curve steeper and the long end higher, re-run EVE and NII under current rates and check the +300 EVE against policy limits; for credit unions the same move feeds the next NEV test and the 5300 call. Measure the move, do not presume its direction.
